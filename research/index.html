  • Journal of Business Logistics · 2026 · 47(2), e70059

    When the Disruptor Is Disrupted: The Role of Transactional Friction on Incumbents During the Exit and Re-Entry of Innovative Firms

    S. Golara, S. Jeong, and Z. S. Rogers

    Examines how competing dealerships adjust inventory when an online retailer exits and re-enters a market. Abstract overview

    Abstract overview

    The study uses Carvana’s license suspensions and reinstatements to examine how established used-car dealerships respond when an online competitor leaves and returns. A synthetic difference-in-differences analysis of monthly sales at 19,281 dealerships finds that nearby competitors gained sales during the suspensions and retained gains after Carvana returned. Greater assortment breadth and higher service levels helped dealers keep the customers they gained.

  • Information Systems Research · 2025 · 36(2), 983–1010

    The Impact of Situational Achievement Goals on Online Learning Behavior: Results from Field Experiments

    N. Mousavi, S. Golara, and J. Bockstedt

    Field experiments show which achievement goals improve participation and performance in an online course. Abstract overview

    Abstract overview

    Two field experiments test how goal-setting prompts affect engagement and performance in a massive open online course. The first experiment involved more than 2,000 learners in 171 countries. Prompts encouraging learners to demonstrate their ability performed especially well, with outcomes varying by prior performance and social activity. The second experiment found the strongest learning outcomes when learners were encouraged both to master the material and to demonstrate their ability.

  • Production and Operations Management · 2024 · 33(9), 1855–1874

    Impact of Inventory Levels and Product Variety on Consumers’ Perceptions of Brands

    K. Rajavi, S. Golara, and S. Modaresi

    Higher vehicle inventory strengthens brand perceptions, and the effect of product variety varies across consumers and brands. Abstract overview

    Abstract overview

    The authors combine vehicle listings from more than 20,000 U.S. dealerships with nearly 274,000 survey responses from people shopping for cars. Across several methods, shoppers perceived brands more favorably when more inventory was available. Assortment breadth had no consistent overall effect on its own. Both effects varied with shopper income and whether the brand was considered a luxury brand.

  • Production and Operations Management · 2024 · 33(3), 663–681

    Service Activity and Patented Innovation in Manufacturing Firms

    S. Golara and K. J. Dooley

    Manufacturers with more service activity generate more patented innovations, especially through services that involve close customer contact. Abstract overview

    Abstract overview

    This study combines three decades of activity at 8,087 manufacturers with records of more than 1.5 million patents. Firms with greater service activity generated more patentable innovations, particularly when their services involved direct customer interaction or specialized knowledge. Additional analyses suggest that some of these patents also departed more sharply from the firms’ earlier innovation patterns.

    Supply Chain Management Research Productivity and Topics: 2020–2022

    M. J. Maloni, R. M. Franza, G. H. Lowman, S. A. Naphsin, and S. Golara

    Tracks who publishes supply chain management research and which topics their studies address. Abstract overview

    Abstract overview

    The authors update a 55-year series on academic publishing in supply chain management by tracking output from 2020 to 2022. Many schools and doctoral programs remained prominent, even as the most productive school changed and research activity became distributed across more scholars. The paper also identifies the research subjects that appeared most often and gives programs a benchmark for evaluating their research activity.

  • Journal of Purchasing and Supply Management · 2024 · 30(1), 100897

    Protect Me Not: The Effect of Tariffs on U.S. Supply Networks

    Z. S. Rogers, S. Golara, Y. Abdulsalam, and D. S. Rogers

    Examines how U.S. steel and aluminum tariffs changed the structure of domestic supply networks. Abstract overview

    Abstract overview

    An event study tracks stock-market responses at 691 U.S. public companies to four groups of tariffs introduced in 2018. It follows producers in the industries targeted for protection along with their suppliers and customers. Firm values fell overall, including for the domestic producers the tariffs aimed to protect, and the effects on their trading partners differed across industries.

  • Journal of Purchasing and Supply Management · 2023 · 29(4), 100858

    Moving Beyond the Four Walls: The Evolving Impact of Supplier Sustainability on Firm Value

    Z. S. Rogers, S. Golara, and C. R. Carter

    Shows how financial markets’ responses to suppliers’ environmental and social performance have changed over time. Abstract overview

    Abstract overview

    The study analyzes 3,986 announcements about social or environmental performance between 1994 and 2013 to compare the effects of companies’ own actions with those of their suppliers. The connection to supplier performance was weak early in the period. After 2000, markets responded to suppliers’ social performance, while suppliers’ environmental performance was less strongly associated with the value of the companies they supplied.

  • The International Journal of Management Education · 2022 · 20(2), 100656

    It’s Not You, It’s Me: Can Others Learn from the Challenges of Teaching Operations and Supply Chain Management?

    J. A. Swaim, M. J. Maloni, S. Golara, and R. Veliyath

    Identifies recurring challenges in teaching operations and supply chain management and offers approaches instructors can use. Abstract overview

    Abstract overview

    The authors survey U.S. operations and supply chain faculty and students taking a core course at one university. The results associate student satisfaction more closely with the individual instructor than with the topics covered or the teaching methods used. Students formed early impressions of instructors that predicted later satisfaction. The study discusses what those patterns mean for teaching courses that students and instructors expect to find difficult.

  • Production and Operations Management · 2021 · 30(10), 3560–3578

    Are Dealers Still Relevant? How Dealer Service Quality Impacts Manufacturer Success

    S. Golara, K. J. Dooley, and N. Mousavi

    Measures how dealership service quality affects the performance of the manufacturers they represent. Abstract overview

    Abstract overview

    The authors combine sales and service-quality ratings for 1,078 U.S. automobile dealerships across five vehicle classes. Better after-sales service was associated with more local sales for the manufacturer, including in markets where its brand already held a strong competitive position. Service during the sale mattered more in competitive markets where a brand had relatively few dealerships.

    Supply Chain Management Research Productivity and Growth: 2017–2019

    M. J. Maloni, S. Golara, and G. H. Lowman

    Measures the growth and distribution of supply chain management research across institutions and journals. Abstract overview

    Abstract overview

    Using six journals, the study benchmarks supply chain management publications from 2017 to 2019 against earlier periods. A core group of highly productive universities remained relatively stable, while rankings outside that group changed more frequently. The analysis also finds a continuing expansion in the number of scholars contributing to the field.

  • Transportation Journal · 2020 · 59(2), 99–128

    A Sharing-Enabled Portfolio Approach to Distribution

    Z. S. Rogers, S. Golara, Y. Abdulsalam, and D. S. Rogers

    Applies a portfolio approach to choosing a mix of shared and dedicated distribution capacity. Abstract overview

    Abstract overview

    Serving customers online and in stores can require distribution facilities with different locations, capacities, and operating models. The paper outlines how shared, on-demand warehousing can complement a firm’s existing facilities as part of a distribution portfolio. It discusses how this flexibility can help firms meet delivery expectations across channels without committing all of their capacity in advance.

  • International Journal of Production Economics · 2018 · 199, 47–64

    Corporate Environmental Investments: A Cross-National Study on Managerial Decision Making

    A. Azadegan, S. Golara, A. Kach, and N. Mousavi

    Compares how managers in different countries make investment decisions under pressure from stakeholders. Abstract overview

    Abstract overview

    A randomized scenario-based survey compares environmental investment decisions among 726 managers in Brazil, China, India, Germany, and the United States. Managers in the developing-country sample responded less to consumer pressure alone. When pressure from the local community accompanied consumer pressure, they were more inclined to invest. Resource availability also affected the two groups differently.

  • International Journal of Information and Decision Sciences · 2013 · 5(4), 331–363

    A Fuzzy-QFD Approach to Balanced Scorecard Using an Analytic Network Process

    M. Tavana, N. Mousavi, and S. Golara

    Develops a method for prioritizing interdependent performance measures in a balanced scorecard. Abstract overview

    Abstract overview

    The authors develop a way to prioritize balanced-scorecard measures when performance objectives are interdependent and expert judgments are imprecise. Quality function deployment maps relationships among the scorecard’s perspectives, an analytic network model accounts for interdependencies, and fuzzy judgments represent uncertainty. A case study illustrates how the combined approach supports performance measurement.

  • International Journal of Strategic Decision Sciences · 2012 · 3(4), 1–26

    Closed-Loop Supply Chain Network Design with Recovery of Glass Containers

    S. Golara, N. Mousavi, M. J. Tarokh, and M. Hosseinzadeh

    Models how to design a supply chain that collects and recovers used glass containers. Abstract overview

    Abstract overview

    This paper develops a mixed-integer optimization model for a beverage supply chain that collects used glass containers. It combines decisions about the network’s design with operational decisions over multiple products and periods, from production and distribution through take-back and reprocessing. The case study illustrates how the model evaluates reuse and recycling alongside the forward flow of products.
